Why this risk exists

The model assigns a 12.5% chance of flash flooding (ROC-AUC = 0.873, indicating strong overall model skill). However, there's an important caveat to discuss.

These are driven by the Active Red Sea Trough (ARST) — a low-level convergence zone over the Red Sea that, when joined by eastward-migrating Mediterranean cyclones, funnels deep moisture toward the southwestern coast (de Vries et al., 2013). Moisture transport from the Red Sea and Arabian Sea further fuels convective development (ResearchGate case study; MDPI Atmosphere, 2019).

⚠️ Important caveat: Model vs. Detection mismatch

The reflexive cross-check flagged a disagreement: the physical detection engine scored the conditions at 0.6 (above its 0.3 threshold), while the trained model gave only 0.125. The note says this is the *riskier mismatch direction* — the physical drivers *were* present, but the model assigned low probability. This warrants caution.

Model agreement & performance

- Ensemble spread: 5 models gave probabilities ranging from 4.3% to 18.5% (std = 0.049), showing moderate disagreement — some members see essentially no risk, others see a non-trivial chance. - Meteorological metrics (at the 0.5 threshold): POD = 0.10, FAR = 0.80, CSI = 0.07. These are low, but that's expected for flash floods — true flash-flood days are extremely rare, so the model's detection rate at a fixed threshold is inherently low.

Bottom line

Low probability (12.5%), but the atmospheric ingredients (high CAPE, moisture, IVT) were present on the preceding day. The physical detection engine saw a signal the model didn't fully capture. Jizan (pop. ~174k) sits at just 8m elevation on the Red Sea coast, making it vulnerable to runoff from the nearby Asir mountains. Recommendation: Monitor conditions closely — the model says low risk, but the raw indicators suggest staying alert for convective development.

    Why Are These Scores So Low?

    Flash floods are extremely rare events. In meteorological verification, rare-event models almost always show low POD and high FAR at their operational threshold because:

    - The vast majority of days are non-event days, so even a small false-alarm rate produces many false alarms relative to the few true events. - The model is calibrated to be conservative — it only triggers at probability ≥ 0.5, which catches the clearest cases but misses many marginal ones.
